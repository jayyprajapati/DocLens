# Study Summary: LUMEN-2 Trial of Nocturnal Light Therapy for Seasonal Low Mood

## Design

LUMEN-2 was a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial run at 6 sites across Norway and Sweden between October 2023 and March 2024. A total of 412 adults aged 18 to 65 with a diagnosis of seasonal affective disorder were randomized 1:1 to active light therapy (10,000 lux for 30 minutes each morning) or a dim red light placebo for 8 weeks.

## Primary outcome

The primary outcome was the change in the SIGH-SAD depression score from baseline to week 8. The active group improved by a mean of 11.4 points and the placebo group by 7.9 points, a difference of 3.5 points (95% CI 1.9 to 5.1; p = 0.002).

## Results by subgroup

| Subgroup | Active group mean improvement | Placebo group mean improvement |
|---|---|---|
| Age 18-39 | 12.1 | 8.3 |
| Age 40-65 | 10.6 | 7.5 |
| Prior antidepressant use | 9.8 | 7.7 |
| No prior antidepressant use | 12.5 | 8.0 |

## Safety

Adverse events were mild. Headache occurred in 14% of the active group and 6% of the placebo group. Eye strain was reported by 9% of the active group and 4% of the placebo group. No serious adverse events were attributed to the treatment. 23 participants withdrew before week 8, 13 from the active group and 10 from the placebo group.

## Limitations

The trial excluded participants with bipolar disorder and those taking photosensitizing medication. Follow-up ended at week 8, so durability beyond two months is unknown. The sites were all in northern Europe, which may limit generalizability to lower latitudes.

## Funding

The study was funded by the Nordic Health Foundation. The authors report no commercial relationships with light-device manufacturers.
